Babies Are Not For Cowards

Are people who embrace risk also more likely to have children?
The Institute for Family Studies asked nearly 10,000 people across the globe. Scroll down to explore the findings.

↓

Countries and groups are positioned along the horizontal axis by their willingness to take risks: the further right, the higher the risk tolerance. Hover over any circle to view its exact score on a 0–10 scale.

Countries are mapped vertically by the mean number of children. Note how lower risk-tolerant countries shift downward, while higher risk-tolerant ones move up.

The survey reveals a positive relationship between risk tolerance and family size: where people are more willing to take risks, fertility rates are noticeably higher.

One clear outlier is Israel, which maintains high fertility despite low risk tolerance. Egypt stands out as well, reporting a substantially higher number of children than any other country in the dataset.

Excluding the two outliers, the relationship between risk tolerance and fertility becomes even stronger.
